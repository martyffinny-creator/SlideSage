Case Study: How a Sales Team Increased Their Close Rate by 23% in 90 Days

✍️ Marcus Roy, Head of Product at SlideSage 📅 March 15, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

GrowthMetrics, a 45-person B2B SaaS company, had a problem. Their product was strong. Their reps were smart. Their pipeline looked healthy on paper. But quarter after quarter, the numbers told a story no one on the leadership team wanted to hear: deals were slipping through the cracks at the demo stage, and nobody could explain why.

That changed when their VP of Sales, Rachel Okonkwo, decided to stop guessing and start looking at the decks.

The Problem: Inconsistency at Scale

GrowthMetrics had ten sales reps, and each one had built their own version of the company pitch deck. Some were polished. Some were twelve-slide masterclasses in burying the lead. A few looked like they were assembled at 11 PM the night before a crucial demo — because they were.

The result was a demo-to-close rate hovering at 18%. Not catastrophic, but well below what the product quality warranted. Management kept cycling through the same theories: pricing objections, competitive pressure, bad-fit leads. None of the fixes landed. The funnel kept leaking.

The real culprit, it turned out, wasn't in the CRM. It was in the slide decks.

The Discovery: Seven Problems, Every Deck

Rachel spent two weeks doing something no one had done before: she manually reviewed every active rep's pitch deck side by side. What she found wasn't random variation — it was a pattern. Across all ten decks, the same seven problems kept surfacing:

  • Slides packed with dense paragraphs that prospects didn't read
  • No clear value proposition in the first three slides
  • Missing or weak social proof — one logo page buried near the end
  • Unclear pricing framing that created sticker shock
  • Weak or absent calls to action at the close
  • Feature-first storytelling instead of outcome-first
  • Slides that didn't visually flow — each one felt like a different presentation

The diagnosis was clear. The treatment was less obvious. Rachel couldn't manually review every deck before every demo — that wasn't scalable. She needed a system.

The Solution: SlideSage as the Mandatory Gate

GrowthMetrics implemented a simple, non-negotiable rule: no rep presents a deck with a SlideSage score below 75.

The workflow was clean. Before any prospect demo, each rep uploaded their deck to SlideSage. The AI analyzed it across content quality, design coherence, narrative structure, and audience alignment. If the score came back below 75, the rep worked through the flagged issues and ran it again. If it hit 75 or above, they were cleared to present.

The 75-point threshold wasn't arbitrary. Rachel set it after running the team's best-performing historical decks through SlideSage and finding they clustered between 77 and 88. It became the baseline for "deck quality that can win."

In the first week, six of ten reps failed their initial check. Average review time dropped significantly as reps learned to front-load the fixes SlideSage consistently flagged. Within three weeks, the team was hitting the threshold on first submission more than 70% of the time.

The confidence score, measured via an internal anonymous survey, stood out to Rachel. "When reps believe in their deck, it shows in the room," she told her leadership team. "That score told us the tool wasn't just improving slides — it was improving the people presenting them."

What They Actually Changed

Across the team's 90-day period, three improvements showed up most consistently in SlideSage's feedback and had the biggest impact on outcomes:

1. Outcome-Led Opening Slides

Every rep rewrote their opening two slides to lead with customer outcomes rather than product features. Instead of "Here's what our platform does," decks opened with "Here's what companies like yours achieve in 60 days." SlideSage flagged feature-first openings as a high-priority issue — fixing this alone moved scores by 8–12 points on average.

2. Social Proof, Earlier and Stronger

The logo wall moved from slide 18 to slide 4. Customer quotes with specific results replaced generic testimonials. SlideSage's narrative structure analysis consistently flagged late-appearing credibility signals as a risk factor — prospects who don't trust you by slide 5 rarely recover.

3. A Single, Clear Next Step

The final slide stopped asking for "any questions?" and started asking for a specific commitment: "Ready to start your 14-day pilot?" Clarity of close-slide CTAs became a standing checklist item for every rep before submission.

"We used to argue about whose deck was better. Now we let the score decide."
— Rachel Okonkwo, VP Sales, GrowthMetrics

Key Takeaways for Your Sales Team

You don't need to be a 45-person SaaS company to apply what GrowthMetrics learned. Here are three things any sales team can implement immediately:

  • Set a minimum quality bar before any rep presents — and make it objective. A score-based threshold removes subjectivity and ego from the conversation.
  • Audit your decks for the same seven problems GrowthMetrics found. Odds are high that text density, weak CTAs, and buried social proof are costing you deals right now.
  • Measure rep confidence, not just conversion. A confident rep with a strong deck is a compounding asset. Track both.

The Scorecard Changes Everything

The most important shift at GrowthMetrics wasn't in the decks — it was in the culture. When quality becomes quantifiable, the conversation changes. Reps stopped defending their slides and started improving them. Managers stopped guessing and started coaching. And prospects started hearing pitches that actually spoke to their problems.

Twenty-three percent doesn't sound like a revolution. But on a sales team closing $2M a year, a 27% relative improvement in close rate is the difference between hitting quota and blowing past it.

The decks were always the variable. Now they have the data to prove it.
