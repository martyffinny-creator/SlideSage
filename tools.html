💡 Tips Library

24 expert tips across 4 categories

Design

Font Consistency

Stick to a maximum of two typefaces throughout your entire deck. Mixing too many fonts creates visual noise and undermines your credibility.

Design

3-Color Palette Rule

Limit yourself to three brand colors: one dominant, one accent, one neutral. More than three makes slides feel chaotic and hard to scan.

Design

Embrace Whitespace

Empty space is not wasted space — it guides the eye and reduces cognitive load. Let your content breathe with generous margins and padding.

Design

One Visual Per Slide

Each slide should contain a single hero image, chart, or diagram that supports the message. Multiple visuals compete for attention and dilute impact.

Design

Align to a Grid

Use your software's alignment and distribution tools to snap elements to an invisible grid. Precise alignment signals professionalism at a subconscious level.

Design

Check Contrast Ratios

Ensure text-to-background contrast meets at least a 4.5:1 ratio for readability. Low contrast forces your audience to squint — and they'll tune out instead.

Content

One Message Per Slide

Every slide should answer one question or make one point. If you can't summarize the slide in a single sentence, it probably needs to be split.

Content

Lead With Data

Anchor key claims with a specific number, study, or case study. Concrete evidence makes arguments memorable and dramatically increases audience trust.

Content

Start With the Problem

Open by articulating a pain point your audience already feels before introducing your solution. This creates immediate emotional buy-in.

Content

End With a Clear CTA

Tell your audience exactly what to do next — schedule a call, sign up, vote yes. A vague "thank you" slide squanders the momentum you built.

Content

Avoid Jargon

Write every slide as if your smartest non-specialist friend will read it. Jargon creates distance; plain language builds connection and comprehension.

Content

Use Active Voice

Write "We increased revenue by 40%" not "Revenue was increased by 40%." Active voice sounds confident, direct, and is 20–30% easier to read quickly.

Delivery

Practice Out Loud

Reading silently is not practicing. Your mouth, pace, and breathing need real rehearsal time to feel natural under the pressure of a live audience.

Delivery

Pause for Emphasis

A two-second pause after a key point feels much longer to you than to your audience. Use deliberate silence — it signals importance and lets ideas land.

Delivery

Make Eye Contact

Pick three or four people in different parts of the room and rotate your gaze between them. This creates the illusion of individual connection with everyone.

Delivery

Use Speaker Notes Wisely

Write your speaker notes as conversational prompts, not scripts. Full sentences encourage reading aloud, which kills engagement instantly.

Delivery

Time Yourself

Record a full run-through and measure each section. Most presenters run 20% over time — knowing this in advance lets you cut before you're on stage.

Delivery

Prepare for Q&A

Anticipate the three hardest questions you might face and rehearse concise answers. Confident Q&A handling often does more for credibility than the main talk.

Structure

Problem–Solution–Proof Arc

The most persuasive presentations follow this three-act structure: establish pain, offer relief, then prove it works. It mirrors how humans naturally make decisions.

Structure

Keep It to 10 Slides

Ten slides force you to ruthlessly prioritize and prevent audience fatigue. If you need more, you likely have a document, not a presentation.

Structure

Use an Agenda Slide

For talks over 15 minutes, a brief agenda slide reduces anxiety and lets your audience relax into the content instead of wondering what's coming next.

Structure

Group Related Topics

Cluster related slides into sections with clear visual transitions. Cognitive chunking makes complex material feel manageable and easier to retain.

Structure

Strong Opener and Closer

Audiences remember the first and last 90 seconds most clearly. Invest disproportionate effort in your opening hook and your closing call-to-action.

Structure

Logical Flow Between Slides

Each slide should feel like a natural consequence of the previous one. If a slide can be removed without disrupting flow, it probably doesn't belong.
